### The eight sorries are one goal

`Dfree (x y z) : op (op (op z x) y) (op x y) = J (op (op z x) y) (op x y)` — "the outer product is
always free". It `rcases TR` into 7 branches and inside each does `rcases (OC B) x (OC C)`; the surviving
cell is always **B decoded, C free**. The file's own comment states it exactly:

> `op z x = oc y` (a subterm of `y`); `B = op (oc y) y` is forced to fire one of TR's own rules, landing
> B on `a1 (a2 y)` / `a2 (a1 (a1 y))` / `oc (oc y)` — all deeper subterms of `y` than `a2 y` itself,
> **but no pure size argument separates that value from `a2 y`; would need a genuine strong induction.**

That is verbatim `PLAYBOOK_PROOF.md` §4.1's shape, and §4 says what closes it: define `Enc`/`RF`, prove
`SND` (one branch per rule, `termination_by msr u v`), the `oD` corollary, a fuel induction giving the
numeric consequence of `Enc`, and `CMP`. Nothing cheaper is known; `rec5837_proof`, `rec12087`,
`rec23354`, `rec24200` and `rec28626` are all stuck on the identical cell.

### What the next agent should do (the design question, stated precisely)

The hole is R1 firing at an intermediate product. Two routes, both used on 13764:

1. **Repair rules** — add rules that still recover `x` when A (or B, or C) decoded, the way 13764's
   W5/W6 do. On 13764 this needed one rule whose guard is a RECURSIVE re-run of the encoding
   (`op (a2 (a2 u)) (a2 u) = a1 v`); no structural approximation of it was sound (three tries).
   Expect the same here, and expect the recursion gate to be provable unconditionally from the
   rule's other guards (13764's `gt` lemma) so the Lean cost is one `let` + one `op_cases`.
2. **Mark the root product with the `E` constructor** so R1 can demand `tg v = 3` and cannot fire
   where `v` is a plain `J`. Sketched and rejected on inspection: the marking rule
   (`tg u = 2 ∧ tg v = 2 ∧ tg (a1 u) = 2 ∧ a2 u = a2 v ∧ a2 (a1 u) = a1 v -> E u v`) itself fires
   at `A = op z x` for suitable `z`, after which `D` is not marked and the root fails. Not
   disproved, but it needs its own repair pass — do not assume it is the cheap route.

**Validate every candidate with the case tree, never with the samplers.** The lab's `validate()`
prints the rule profile of every chain; a profile whose intermediate products are all `F` means the
sweep has not exercised the interesting cells at all, which is exactly the state the single-rule
baseline is in. Construct one instance per cell by chained encoding, as `CE1()` does.

### The three design facts, each measured

1. **No guard may depend on A.** A is fed by the junk variable `z` and marks or decodes freely.
   K1 checked `a2 (a1 u) = a1 v` and paid 15,536 fails; dropping it (K2) took the level-k descent
   from 5,425 fails to **0**. This is the single most important constraint and it is specific to
   12234's shape (13764 had no junk variable in that position).
2. **The mark must keep BOTH arguments.** K1/K2 emit `E (a1 v) (a2 v)`, discarding `u`; K4 emits
   `E u v` and drops 22,808 -> 1,494. The reason is structural: with `E u v`, a mark that misfires
   at B or C is still *transparent* — its `a2` is unchanged, so the D product still marks and the
   root still reads through it. Profiles `M,F,F,M,R`, `F,F,M,M,R`, `F,M,F,M,R`, `M,F,M,M,R` all
   succeed under K4+, i.e. marks at A, B and C are each tolerated.
3. **R must certify that `v` was produced by M** — `tg (a1 v) ≠ 1 ∧ tg (a2 v) ≠ 1` is M's own guard

# CARRIER PASS 3 — the `tg u = 1` cell resists, and the failures form a pattern

Three attempts at K18's last cell, all following the coordinator's "recompute, do not test shape"
principle (12087's `Dec`/`P`). All three used a genuine recomputation; all three regressed.

| carrier | rules | design for the cell | HARD battery | what broke |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **K18** (baseline) | 4 | — (cell open) | **16** | H5 only |
| K19 | 6 | `M4` mark + `R3` relaxed reading | 7,390 | **R3 forges at A** (H3 7,060) |
| K20 | 5 | `M4` emits `E v v` so plain `R` reads it | 32,518 | **M4 eats the payload at C** (H6 21,323) |
| K21 | 6 | `M4` emits `E u v` + narrow reading `R4` gated `tg u != 1` | 52 | **R4 forges at B** (H2/H6 47) |

**The recomputation guard works.** K20 and K21 both score **H3 = 0 / 689,976** — the family that
destroys K15 (100 % fail) and K19 (7,060). So the coordinator's principle is confirmed: a guard that
re-runs a product cannot be forged by H3, because H3 forges shape, not evaluation. K20 also confirms
design fact 2 from the other side: it is the ONLY carrier here that discards an argument (`E v v`),
and it is the only one whose *marks* misfire destructively (21,323 in H6). Every other carrier's mark
misfires are harmless.

**But every fix trades one hole for another, and the pattern is sharp: all three regressions are
READING rules, never marks.** `R3` fires at A, `R4` fires at B, and the mark `M4` is harmless in
both K19 and K21. A mark that keeps `E u v` is transparent by construction (design fact 2), so
adding marks is nearly free; a *reading* rule commits to an answer and is therefore forgeable at
every position whose `(u, v)` can be made to match. That is the conservation law here:

> **Adding a mark is cheap; adding a reading is expensive. Each new reading rule is a new forge
> surface at all four inner positions, and H3/H2 will find it.**

So the cell wants a guard that is *not* a new reading — either a strengthening of `R` that admits
`tg (a1 v) = 1` without a new rule, or a mark that repairs `a1 v` so plain `R` still applies (K20's
idea, which failed only because it discarded `u`; a mark emitting `E <something with a2 = a2 v> v`
rather than `E v v` was not tried and is the obvious next candidate).

## THE IMPOSSIBILITY ARGUMENT (at this level)

**The cell.** `A` and `B` both decode and `B` lands on a generator; `D = op B C` must be marked for
the root to read anything, because every reading requires `tg v = 3`.

**Step 1 — the mark must fire with `tg u = 1`.** Forced: `u = B` is a generator. K23 shows a rule
that does this exists and is free of side effects.

**Step 2 — a VERBATIM mark at this cell is unreadable, provably.** A verbatim mark gives
`a1 D = B` and `a2 D = C`. The root's reading `R` requires `a2 (a1 v) = a2 (a2 v)`. With `a1 v = B`
a generator, `a2 (a1 v) = a2 B = B` (accessors are total: `a2` of a generator is itself), while
`a2 (a2 v) = a2 C = y`. So `R` applies **iff `B = y`** — and `B` is a generator while **`y` is
provably not one in this cell**: if `y` were a generator no rule could fire on `op _ y` at all
(every rule needs `tg v = 3` or `tg v != 1`), so `B` and `C` would both be free and plain `M` would
have marked `D`. Hence `B != y` and `R` can never read it. Confirmed empirically: K23's
`R,R,M,M6,F`.

**Step 3 — so exactly one of two things must change, and both are measured expensive.**

| escape | what it is | measured | why it fails |

| (a) non-verbatim mark | make `a1 D` not the generator | K20 **32,518**, K22 **32,484** | such a mark also fires at the **C** position, where it wraps `a1 (a2 v)` — the slot the root reads |

Both levers are exhausted, in both directions, with the recomputation principle applied throughout
